- Problem it solves: `review` reported accurate status (stagnant metrics, open
  tracks) but left the prioritising to the reader, who is usually already deepest
  in one branch and least able to notice they should be elsewhere. Whole tracks
  (an unwired alignment script, a corpus-wide scan with no consumer) went
  unmeasured and un-iterated as a result, invisible to `review` because they had
  no task entry at all.
- What was added there: a `== Proposed next step ==` section at the end of
  `review` — one recommended task, a stated reason, and a goal. Ranking is by
  leverage (`blocks: [task_id, ...]`, how many other tracks depend on this one)
  then cost (`cheap` / `session` / `multi-session`), never by momentum; tracks
  with no metric get a ranking bonus because being unjudgeable is itself a defect
  (per `iteration-policy.md`). New task fields: `next_action`, `goal`, `cost`,
  `blocks` (set via `svz.py update`); `next_action` falls back to parsing a
  "Next action:" sentence out of existing notes. `doctor` validates the new
  fields. Tests in `tests/test_svz_proposal.py`.
- **Scope relative to the existing orchestrator above**: that one (`workflow_mcp`,
  `plans/iteration.toml`) is a *within-track* polish/continue-stop gate. This is a
  *cross-track* strategic pick — which track gets the next session at all. They
  compose, they don't overlap; reconcile wording between `review`'s closing
  message here and `template/scripts/svz.py`'s (this repo says
  "docs/wisdom/iteration-policy.md (or the project's copy)"; the drifted copy
  says "docs/ITERATION_POLICY.md") when doing the actual port.
